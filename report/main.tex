\section{Executive Summary}

\subsection*{Business Need and Opportunity}

Luckin Coffee currently manages its sustainability data --- including carbon emissions across thousands of stores, waste-sorting compliance, and ESG-reporting workflows --- through a fragmented set of spreadsheets and stand-alone reports maintained by individual departments. This fragmentation creates three concrete pain points: (i)~the GRI~Standards and ISO~14064-aligned annual sustainability report takes more than three weeks of manual consolidation; (ii)~tightening external regulations (UN~SDG~12 reporting expectations and the latest ESG disclosure requirements) increase the risk of non-compliance penalties; and (iii)~store-level energy and waste decisions cannot be supported by real-time data. Resolving these issues is both a regulatory necessity and a business opportunity: a unified platform is expected to compress reporting cycles, reduce compliance risk, and enable data-driven operational decisions across the retail network.

\subsection*{Project Objectives}

This project delivers the \textit{Business Sustainability Management Platform} (hereafter the \textit{Luckin Coffee Sustainability Platform}) --- a web-based ESG-management system covering ten functional modules organised under three core business areas: \textbf{Carbon-Emission Tracking}, \textbf{Waste Management}, and \textbf{Sustainability Reporting}. The Platform is built on a Flask blueprint architecture (Python backend), a Vue~3 + Bootstrap frontend, and a MySQL database, deployed to a cloud virtual machine procured from a qualified Luckin~Coffee supplier and integrated with the DeepSeek~API for natural-language report drafting. The Platform replaces the existing manual workflow and provides a single source of truth for all sustainability data.

\subsection*{Quantitative Success Criteria}

The project's success is measured against the following stakeholder-approved acceptance criteria, agreed at the M1 milestone gate and consistent with the Project Charter:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Scope}: all ten functional modules pass user-acceptance testing with no unresolved severe defects; system documentation and user manuals fully delivered.
    \item \textbf{Schedule}: project completed within the 16-week timeline (March--June~2026) with all six milestone gates (M1--M6) accepted on or before their forecast dates listed in Table~\ref{tab:milestone_schedule}.
    \item \textbf{Cost}: total expenditure remains within the Cost Baseline of \textbf{¥1{,}200{,}000} (BAC); a Cost Performance Index (CPI) of at least 0.9 maintained over any four-week window.
    \item \textbf{Quality}: defect density at or below 3~defects per 1{,}000~LOC; backend API average response time under 2~seconds; functional test pass rate at least 95\% before each milestone gate.
    \item \textbf{Business value}: end-to-end sustainability-report generation time reduced from three weeks to under one week after deployment, validated through a post-deployment benchmark conducted by the Luckin~Coffee ESG Department.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Delivery Approach Summary}

The project is delivered through an enterprise-style delivery organisation centred on a four-member project management and development core. The core team covers five specialist responsibilities across architecture, backend, frontend, visualisation, and quality through assigned work-package roles, supported by an external ESG-compliance consultant engaged for Weeks~2--4 and two client-side representatives: the Head of Luckin~Coffee IT Department as project sponsor and the Head of Luckin~Coffee ESG Department as business owner. Management is conducted under a strong matrix organisational structure with the Project Management Information System assembled from GitHub for version control, WeChat for real-time communication, Google Docs for collaborative documentation, and MySQL Workbench for database management. The project applies the PMBOK 6th~Edition process model (R1) across all ten knowledge areas covered in Sections~2--11 of this plan, with Earned~Value~Management (EVM) used for cost and schedule control at every weekly review and every milestone gate.

\subsection*{Top Risks and Reserves}

Fifteen risks (twelve threats and three opportunities) have been identified through brainstorming, SWOT, structured interviews, and root-cause analysis, organised under a Risk~Breakdown~Structure with three top-level categories (Technical, External, Organizational). The five highest-priority risks --- customer scope expansion (E1), cross-module integration defects (T3), architecture instability under load (T1), ESG-regulatory updates (E3), and team-member partial reallocation (O2) --- carry an aggregated Expected~Monetary~Value of \textit{minus} ¥86{,}000. The cost baseline includes a \textbf{¥120{,}000 contingency reserve} (10\% of BAC) and the sponsor controls an additional \textbf{¥80{,}000 management reserve} outside the baseline. The schedule baseline carries a 15-working-day contingency reserve and an 8-working-day management reserve.

\subsection*{Document Roadmap}

Sections~2--3 describe how scope is defined and integrated; Sections~4--5 establish the schedule and cost baselines; Section~6 specifies quality standards and metrics; Section~7 details the process-improvement approach; Section~8 covers human-resource acquisition and team development; Section~9 governs procurement and contracts; Section~10 specifies the communications and stakeholder-engagement strategy; Section~11 presents the full risk-management plan including identification, qualitative and quantitative analysis, response strategies, and control; and Section~12 lists all supporting appendices and normative references.

\section{Integration Management}
This section includes all of the processes required to unify, coordinate and manage all project elements to completion for the \textit{Business Sustainability Management Platform} developed by the project team.


\subsection{Integration Management Overview and Approach}
The primary objective of integration management for this project is to coordinate six work packages and unify ten functional modules to deliver a fully functional web-based ESG management platform. The integration strategy employs a modular blueprint architecture implemented in Flask. This ensures each business module, such as carbon tracking and waste management, operates independently while sharing a unified data layer, authentication system, and frontend framework. All subsidiary plans are integrated into this comprehensive project management plan. The scope baseline encompasses the ten functional modules detailed in the System Documentation. The schedule baseline aligns with a 16-week timeline from March to June, featuring monthly milestones for design, development, and testing.

\subsection{Project Team Structure and Governance}

The internal team consists of 4 members, supported by the project sponsor and the \textbf{IT department providing virtual machine hosting}. External stakeholders include compliance standard bodies like GRI and ISO 14064, open-source software communities, and target end-users. Communication is maintained through weekly progress reports submitted to the sponsor, ensuring adherence to GRI Standards and UN SDG 12. The corporate governance structure, illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:team_structure}, involves a Project Steering Committee that conducts weekly reviews and cross-module coordination.

\subsection{Direct and Manage Project Execution}
Execution activities are driven by a structured workflow where all code and documentation are managed via GitHub. A main branch is maintained for stable releases alongside feature branches for specific work packages, with peer reviews required before merging. Weekly synchronization meetings are held every Monday to review progress, identify blockers, and align upcoming tasks. Work performance data is collected weekly and integrated into status reports, including completed tasks, bug resolution rates, and test coverage. Approved change requests are assigned to relevant work package leads, and their implementation is verified by the project manager before closure. The Project Management Information System (PMIS) comprises GitHub for version control, Wechat for real-time communication, Google Docs for collaborative documentation, and MySQL Workbench for database management.

\subsection{Monitor and Control Project Work}
Project performance is continuously monitored against the 16-week schedule baseline. Key performance indicators include schedule variance, defect density, and cross-module integration resolution time. Earned Value Management (EVM) principles are applied to quantify progress, triggering corrective actions if the Schedule Performance Index (SPI) falls below 0.9 for two consecutive weeks. A three-tiered information flow ensures consistent reporting: raw work performance data is analyzed into performance information, which is then compiled into weekly status reports for the team and monthly updates for the sponsor. When deviations occur, the root cause is analyzed to develop corrective actions that minimize schedule disruption. Furthermore, technical risks and data anomalies are tracked during weekly reviews, utilizing the platform's built-in anomaly detection module to maintain data integrity.

\subsection{Change Management}

\subsubsection{Change Governance}
The formal change control process governs all modifications affecting the project's scope, schedule, or quality baselines. The Change Control Board (CCB) is the central governing body responsible for reviewing, evaluating, approving, delaying, or rejecting changes to the project. The CCB is chaired by the Project Manager and includes the Project Sponsor and Work Package Leads.

\subsubsection{Change Identification and Request Management}
Any team member or stakeholder can identify a need for a change. Once identified, the change must be formally documented via a standardized GitLab Issue template, which serves as the formal Change Request (CR). The CR must include a clear description of the proposed change, the rationale behind it, and the expected benefits.

\subsubsection{Impact Analysis}
Upon submission of a Change Request, the relevant Work Package Lead conducts a comprehensive impact analysis. This analysis evaluates the potential effects of the change on the project's scope, schedule, budget, quality, and risks. The impact analysis results are appended to the GitLab Issue to provide the CCB with sufficient information for decision-making.

\subsubsection{Change Approval Process}
The CCB reviews pending change requests during weekly Wednesday meetings. The approval authority depends on the change's magnitude:
As for minor changes, defect repairs or minor adjustments with negligible impact are approved directly by the Project Manager.
As for major changes, scope additions or changes significantly affecting the schedule or budget require authorization from the Project Sponsor.
As for emergency changes, critical bug fixes may be implemented immediately to restore system functionality but must undergo retrospective CCB review within three business days.

\subsubsection{Change Tracking}
All change requests, regardless of their approval status, are tracked in a living Change Log maintained within GitLab. The Change Log records the CR status (e.g., Submitted, Under Review, Approved, Rejected, Implemented), the approver, and the implementation date. This ensures traceability and guarantees that only approved modifications are integrated into the main codebase.

The Change Control Board is structured to ensure comprehensive evaluation of change requests from both technical and business perspectives.

\subsection{Project Close Out}
The project close-out phase ensures a systematic transition of the final deliverables to the sponsor. This process begins with scope verification to confirm all baseline requirements are met, followed by a final system quality acceptance test. Upon successful validation, the fully functional platform, comprehensive system documentation, user manuals, and presentation materials are formally transitioned to the sponsor, culminating in a signed acceptance certificate. Finally, a post-mortem meeting is conducted to document lessons learned, evaluating the effectiveness of the modular architecture and team structure to update organizational process assets.

\subsection{Requirements Management}
Requirements are systematically categorized into business, stakeholder, solution, transition, project, and quality requirements. Business requirements focus on centralized ESG data management and compliance with GRI Standards. Stakeholder needs range from simple data entry for store staff to comprehensive analytics for headquarters managers. Solution requirements dictate the functional modules, such as carbon tracking and supplier assessment, alongside non-functional targets like supporting 1000 concurrent users and ensuring sub-two-second page load times. These requirements are elicited through interviews with restaurant managers, focus groups, prototyping, and document analysis. Prioritization follows the MoSCoW method, distinguishing essential features like carbon tracking from optional enhancements like OCR-assisted input. A Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) is maintained to link each requirement to its source, WBS component, and test case, ensuring comprehensive coverage and facilitating impact analysis during change requests.

\subsection{Project Scope Statement}
The primary objective is to develop and deploy a web-based Business Sustainability Management Platform for multi-store restaurant chains by June 30, 2026. The product scope encompasses core modules for carbon tracking, waste management, and ESG governance, featuring role-based access control. The project scope includes all design, development, testing, deployment, and documentation activities. Out-of-scope items include native mobile application development, real-time IoT sensor integration, legacy data migration, and long-term post-delivery maintenance. Deliverables are accepted based on the successful implementation of all functional requirements, resolution of critical bugs, and successful deployment on the designated virtual machine. The project is constrained by a fixed June 2026 deadline, a four-member team, and the mandatory use of the Flask and MySQL technology stack, assuming continuous availability of team members and virtual machine infrastructure.

\subsection{Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)}

\subsubsection{Corrective Action When Performance Deviates}

When actual progress deviates from the planned schedule, corrective and preventive actions are applied according to the severity of the deviation. The primary recovery tools are two schedule compression techniques---crashing and fast tracking.

\textbf{Minor deviation} (individual tasks delayed by 1--3 days, with no impact on the critical path): The affected work package lead is required to promptly report the delay. Upon receiving the notification, the project manager initiates an ad hoc coordination meeting to review the cause and reallocate tasks within the team if necessary. No formal change request is required.

\textbf{Moderate deviation} (a critical-path task at risk, or SPI $<$ 0.9 for two consecutive weeks): A corrective action review is convened within 48 hours. The project manager and relevant work package leads conduct a root cause analysis. Two schedule compression techniques are applied as appropriate:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Fast tracking}: Activities originally planned sequentially are performed in parallel for part of their duration---for example, conducting integration and unit testing while development is still in progress. Fast tracking may introduce rework risk and is applied only when task overlap is technically feasible.
    \item \textbf{Crashing}: Additional resources are assigned to activities to shorten their duration---for example, reallocating a team member from a lower-priority work package or temporarily engaging outsourced developers. Crashing may increase project costs and coordination overhead, so it is applied selectively with careful cost control.
\end{itemize}

Contingency reserves built into the schedule baseline may be drawn upon to absorb moderate delays without triggering a formal baseline revision.

\textbf{Severe deviation} (a milestone missed, or SPI $<$ 0.9 for four or more consecutive weeks): Failure to achieve any milestone is considered a severe deviation and requires a formal schedule revision to be initiated.

\subsubsection{Schedule Modification and Commitment}

The schedule baseline may be formally revised when a milestone is missed, when an approved scope change materially affects the critical path, or when a verified technical blocker cannot be absorbed by available float or contingency reserve. The project manager prepares the revised schedule in Jira, presents it at the Wednesday CCB meeting, and obtains approval per the authority levels in Section 2.6. Each work package lead acknowledges revised task dates within 24 hours via Jira comment. The previous baseline is archived in GitLab as a tagged release. Formal revisions are limited to three over the project lifecycle.

\subsection{Control Quality}
% ============================================================

Quality Control focuses on verifying whether project deliverables meet the required quality standards. It is mainly performed during the execution and closing phases to demonstrate that the completed work satisfies the defined acceptance criteria.

\subsubsection{Quality Control Activities}

The project applies multiple quality control mechanisms throughout development and testing:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Unit Testing}: backend APIs, business logic, and frontend components are tested individually before integration.
    \item \textbf{Integration Testing}: interfaces between frontend modules, backend services, and the database are validated after module integration.
    \item \textbf{System Testing}: the complete platform is tested against all functional and non-functional requirements.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Browser and Cross-Device Testing}: compatibility is validated across the supported browsers and screen resolutions defined during planning.
    \item \textbf{Performance Testing}: database queries and API response times are measured and optimised before deployment.
    \item \textbf{User Acceptance Testing (UAT)}: the client representative validates that the platform satisfies operational and business requirements before final acceptance.
    \item \textbf{Peer Review and Inspection}: source code, technical documentation, and testing artifacts are reviewed before milestone approval.
\end{itemize}

All identified defects are recorded in Jira, assigned to responsible developers, prioritized according to severity, and tracked until closure.

\subsubsection{Defect Reporting and Corrective Action}

Defects are classified into three severity levels:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Minor defects}: cosmetic or low-impact issues that do not affect core functionality. These are corrected during normal development activities.
    \item \textbf{Moderate defects}: issues affecting business workflows or integration behavior. These require corrective action before milestone approval.
    \item \textbf{Severe defects}: critical failures affecting system stability, security, or major business functions. These trigger immediate escalation to the project manager and may delay milestone acceptance until resolved.
\end{itemize}

If defect trends indicate recurring process problems, the project manager initiates a root cause analysis using quality management tools such as Pareto analysis or cause-and-effect diagrams. Corrective actions may include additional testing, process adjustments, code refactoring, or reassignment of technical resources.

\subsubsection{Verified Deliverables and Acceptance}

A deliverable is considered verified only when:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item all planned testing activities are completed,
    \item no unresolved severe defects remain open,
    \item milestone acceptance criteria are satisfied, and
    \item the responsible reviewer formally approves the deliverable in Jira or GitLab.
\end{itemize}

Verified deliverables are then submitted for milestone acceptance review by the Project Manager, Client Representative, or Sponsor.

Risk Management for the Luckin Coffee Sustainability Platform is structured around the six processes defined in PMBOK: \textbf{Plan Risk Management}, \textbf{Identify Risks}, \textbf{Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis}, \textbf{Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis}, \textbf{Plan Risk Responses}, and \textbf{Control Risks}. \subsection{Plan Risk Management}

The Risk Management Plan defines how risk activities will be conducted on this project. It is structured around seven planning elements: methodology, roles and responsibilities, budgeting, timing, risk categories, definitions of probability and impact, and the probability and impact matrix.

\subsubsection{Methodology, Roles, and Timing}

\textbf{Methodology.} Risk management on this project follows the PMBOK process model (R1) and established software project management practice (R2). The team applies the following tools and techniques across the six processes: brainstorming, SWOT analysis, structured interviews, and root cause analysis for identification; the Probability and Impact (P--I) Matrix for qualitative analysis; Expected Monetary Value (EMV) analysis with decision tree and Sensitivity Analysis (Tornado Diagram) for quantitative analysis; the eight PMBOK response strategies for response planning; and risk reassessment, risk audits, variance and trend analysis, technical performance measurement, reserve analysis, and meetings for control. All risk records are maintained in a Risk Register stored in Confluence and version-controlled in GitLab.

\textbf{Roles and Responsibilities.} The Project Manager is the overall risk owner, accountable for risk planning, escalation, and reserve approval below the management threshold. Each work package lead is the primary risk owner for risks within their scope and is responsible for monitoring triggers and executing approved response actions. Every team member is required to identify and report new risks during the daily stand-up. The ESG Compliance Consultant supports identification and analysis of compliance-related risks during Weeks~2--4. The Project Sponsor (Head of Luckin Coffee IT Department) approves use of the management reserve and authorises responses to severe risks beyond the project manager's authority.

\textbf{Timing.} Risk management activities are integrated into the project rhythm: a five-minute risk check is included in every daily stand-up; the Risk Register is reviewed and updated during every Friday team meeting (15 minutes); a full risk reassessment, including P--I matrix re-scoring, is conducted at every milestone gate (M1--M6); a formal risk audit is performed at the M3 and M5 gates by an independent reviewer.

\textbf{Effort Allocation.} Approximately 5\% of total project effort is budgeted for risk management activities, distributed across identification, analysis, response planning, and control.

\subsubsection{Risk Budgeting}

Risk budgeting is integrated with the cost and schedule baselines defined in Sections~4 and~5. Two reserve layers are maintained:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Contingency Reserve} (within the cost and schedule baselines, managed by the Project Manager): covers identified ``known-unknown'' risks listed in the Risk Register. Schedule contingency is 15~working days, as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:estimate_duration}; cost contingency is sized to cover the aggregate Expected Monetary Value of negative risks plus a 20\% safety margin (see Section~11.4). The Project Manager may draw on this reserve without sponsor approval, provided the use is documented in the Risk Register and reported at the next milestone gate.
    \item \textbf{Management Reserve} (outside the baselines, controlled by the Project Sponsor): covers ``unknown-unknown'' risks that cannot be identified at planning time. Schedule management reserve is 8~working days. Use of the management reserve requires a formal change request approved by the sponsor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identify Risks}

Risk identification is conducted iteratively throughout the project lifecycle. The team applies the following four information-gathering and analysis techniques:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Brainstorming}: the entire team holds a structured brainstorming session at project kickoff and at the start of each major phase. Risks are captured without judgement, then grouped against the RBS categories.
    \item \textbf{SWOT Analysis}: applied during the planning phase to systematically examine project Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. Threats and Weaknesses translate into negative risks; Opportunities translate into positive risks (handled with exploit/enhance/share strategies, see Section~11.5).
    \item \textbf{Interviews}: structured interviews are conducted with the Head of Luckin Coffee IT Department, the Head of Luckin Coffee ESG Department, and the external ESG Compliance Consultant during Week~1, focusing on customer expectations, regulatory updates, and historical project pain points.
    \item \textbf{Root Cause Analysis}: applied to recurring defects and identified risks during the execution phase, using the 5-Why method and the fishbone diagram introduced in Section~7. The output feeds back into the Risk Register as updated triggers and improved response plans.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis}

The sensitivity analysis confirms that customer-driven scope expansion (E1) and integration defects (T3) are the dominant drivers of cost uncertainty, justifying the prioritised response strategies in Section~11.5.

\subsection{Control Risks}

Risk control is performed throughout the project lifecycle using the following tools and techniques:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Risk Reassessment}: the Risk Register is reassessed at every Friday team meeting (15~minutes) and at every milestone gate. New risks are added; closed risks are archived; P--I scores of remaining risks are updated based on latest information.
    \item \textbf{Risk Audits}: an independent reviewer (the Quality Manager from Luckin Coffee's IT Department) conducts a formal risk audit at the M3 and M5 gates, examining the effectiveness of risk responses and the discipline of risk identification. Audit findings are documented in the Risk Register.
    \item \textbf{Variance and Trend Analysis}: weekly Cost Performance Index (CPI) and Schedule Performance Index (SPI), defined in Section~5.3, are tracked as risk indicators. A two-week trend with CPI or SPI $<$0.9 triggers a focused risk reassessment, since negative cost or schedule trends often signal an emerging risk.
    \item \textbf{Technical Performance Measurement}: technical metrics including API response time, page load time, integration test pass rate, and defect density (defined in Section~6.1) are measured at each milestone gate. Underperformance against targets is escalated as a potential risk trigger.
    \item \textbf{Reserve Analysis}: the remaining contingency reserve (in CNY and working days) is reviewed weekly. If reserve consumption exceeds the linear schedule (e.g., 40\% of reserve consumed by M2 when only 25\% of work is complete), the Project Manager initiates a formal corrective review with the sponsor.
    \item \textbf{Status Meetings}: risk status follows the communication rhythm defined in Section~11.1, with focused review at every Friday team meeting and every milestone review with Luckin Coffee.
\end{enumerate}

The Risk Register is the single source of truth for all risk control activities, maintained in Confluence with version control via GitLab. Every approved response action, every triggered contingent plan, and every reserve drawdown is recorded against its risk ID for end-of-project lessons-learned analysis.
